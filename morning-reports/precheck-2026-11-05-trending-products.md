## Twelve paintings, and the signal behind each

### 1. Personalized night-sky star map (one date, one place)

A round or rectangular chart of the sky on a night the buyer names, with the place and a short line (“the night we met,” a wedding, a birth).

Why it is moving: [The Night Sky](https://www.thenightsky.com/shop/star-map-print) sells this as the product. The page checked today shows the print at $39 and, in the care section, 42,050 reviews for the company. That count is the brand, not a unit total for one poster. The UK wedding page in search results was £35 with 3,950 reviews on that page. [Under Lucky Stars](https://support.underluckystars.com/hc/en-us/articles/360022352254-How-to-order-what-are-the-prices) publishes US unframed prices, article updated May 19, 2026: $130 for 12×16, $195 for 18×24, $250 for 24×36, frames about $45–$70 more, digital $29. A direct open of that help page timed out, so those figures are from the search extract of that URL. On Etsy, [LdotA’s couples star map](https://www.etsy.com/uk/listing/1457465134/personalised-star-map-print-couples) carries Etsy’s bestseller line (“high sales volume over the past 6 months”). Listed prices there: digital £5.40, 8×10 £15, 18×24 £34.80, 24×36 £50.40. The [Etsy star-map market](https://www.etsy.com/market/star_map_print) is dominated by the same gift wording, with printed posters advertised from about $10 to $35 and some digital files near $4–$6. Parentheses on those market cards are shop review counts, not listing sales, so they are not used here as units sold.

Price range: about $4–$15 for a digital file, about $15–$60 for an unframed print on Etsy and at The Night Sky’s $39 entry, and $130–$250 for a premium unframed poster at Under Lucky Stars. Common print sizes in those listings: 8×10, 11×14, 12×16, 16×20, 18×24, 24×36.

Target buyer: someone buying a gift for a wedding, anniversary, engagement, or a birth. The listing has to show a finished example, because the buyer cannot see their own sky until it is made.

Risk: this is a custom job. A static Printify poster cannot do it. Someone has to take the date, time, and place, send a proof, and only then print. Star positions are facts; the layout of The Night Sky or Under Lucky Stars is their design and is not ours to copy. A map that is only decorative must not be labeled scientifically verified. [A June 2026 price comparison by Starry Memory](https://starry-memory.com/custom-star-map-comparison), a seller in this market, says several shops run a permanent “sale” off an inflated anchor price. Compare the price the buyer actually pays. Etsy digital files at a few dollars are the price pressure on a $39.99 physical poster.

Already planned: no.

### 2. The moon on one named night

One disc, the phase as it appeared on a date the buyer cares about, with the date printed under it. Across the room it looks like a moon print. Up close it is a record of a night.

Why it is a separate painting from the eight-phase chart: [Trendsy Shop’s moon-phase guide](https://trendsyshop.com/blog/moon-phase-wall-art) splits “moon phase art” into four products. A single dated moon is the gift. An eight-disc chart is a diagram. Their own canvas prices on that page: 12×16 at $49.99, 16×20 at $69.99, 20×28 at $99, 24×32 at $119.99. They argue a moon needs a larger size because it is mostly empty space. That is one seller’s sizing advice, not a market census.

Price range: about $50–$120 for a canvas at that shop. A paper print in the same gift slot as the star maps above would sit nearer $20–$40 if it matches Etsy star-map print prices. No independent unit count was found for this exact painting.

Target buyer: the same gift buyer as the star map, especially a birth or a wedding, and a bedroom wall that wants one moon rather than a chart.

Risk: if the phase is wrong for the date, the gift fails. Do not use a NASA photograph; the repo rule already bars that. A black disc on a black field prints as a hole if the file has no texture. Personalized proof step, same as the star map.

Already planned: no. The live Moon Phase product is the eight-disc chart, not a single dated moon.

### 3. Eight-phase moon chart

Eight discs, new through full and back, as one painting.

Why it still belongs on the page: it is the diagram people search under “moon phase.” [Lone Star Art’s 18×24 unframed “Astronomy Chart Phases Of The Moon”](https://www.amazon.com/Poster-Vintage-Star-Historical-Art-Renaissance/dp/B0DDLFLNCF) was $36.99 in search results, rated 4.2 from 84 reviews. The matching 11×14 framed version was $34.99. A seller guide, [Elistit, May 30, 2026](https://elistit.com/guides/celestial-wall-art-etsy/), calls the eight-phase row the anchor of the celestial niche and warns that eight plain white circles on black no longer stand out. That guide does not publish sales counts. Cheap Amazon charts (laminated classroom posters around $10–$17 in the same search) are a different object: a school chart, not a painting.

Price range for an art poster: about $15–$37 unframed at 11×14 to 18×24, from the Lone Star Art listings and the live $39.99. Classroom laminates sit near $5–$17 and are not the comp for this page.

Target buyer: a bedroom, study, or living room that wants a lunar diagram. Broader than a zodiac sign, because the buyer does not have to match a birthday.

Risk: the live painting has to stay visually specific (the repo file is an original eight-disc drawing on warm paper, which is the right direction). Matching a $10 laminate price would sit under the repo’s unconfirmed landed cost. “Educational” claims are fine as a description of a diagram; medical or astrology-prediction claims are not this product.

Already planned: yes. Live as Moon Phase, 18×24, $39.99.

### 4. Year moon poster (every night of one year)

A grid of small moons for a whole year, hung like a calendar and kept as a print.

Why the search volume is real, with a dating caveat: Amazon’s [lunar calendar results](https://www.amazon.com/lunar-calendar/s?k=lunar+calendar), as returned on this check, list a “Moon Calendar 2027” hangable poster at $9.99 with 382 reviews and a “100+ bought in past month” badge, and a 12×24 inch 2027 wall calendar with moon phases at $9.99 with 910 reviews and a “400+ bought” badge. Those same results still say delivery in September and that a Rizzoli Lunar 2027 calendar “will be released on September 22, 2026.” Today is November 5, 2026, so that page is stale. The motif is established. The badge counts are not a November sales figure.

Price range: about $10–$20 for a paper calendar or poster in those listings. A fine-art POD version at $39.99 would be a different price band from the thing that carries the badges.

Target buyer: a moon-tracker gift in late autumn, for a kitchen or office, bought as a 2027 calendar rather than as a permanent painting.

Risk: it expires. A 2027 grid is dead in January 2028. POD can reprint a new year without inventory, which fits the no-stock rule, but the art has to be redrawn each year. The $10 Amazon poster is the price buyers are shown first.

Already planned: no.

### 5. Full northern sky chart (the whole disc, not one animal)

One poster of the northern constellations together: a restored antique chart or a clean modern chart of all the figures, wide enough to read as a map.

Why it is the stronger constellation painting: shoppers searching “star map poster” are shown full charts. Etsy’s market cards include equatorial and northern/southern charts, and vintage restored charts such as [Capricorn Press’s 1849 northern map with Orion](https://www.etsy.com/listing/684894693/vintage-star-map-print-with-orion), sold as a fine-art print, a framed print, or a canvas, with a 24×36 crop added because frames exist in that size. A single beast from *Urania’s Mirror* is a detail of this category. Sets of all thirty Hall plates are also sold as instant downloads around $4, for example [this 30-print Urania’s Mirror set](https://www.etsy.com/listing/1649711638/vintage-star-map-art-set-of-30-uranias), which had almost no reviews in the snippet. The download price, not the review count, is the pressure.

Price range: unframed vintage-style charts on Amazon search results were about $15–$30 for 12×18 to 16×24. Etsy fine-art prints in this family sit higher once framed. A specific bestseller unit count for one full-sky painting was not verified in this pass.

Target buyer: a study, library, or dark-academia wall. [Elistit’s dark-academia guide](https://elistit.com/guides/dark-academia-wall-art-etsy/) says a historically specific chart (“1692 celestial atlas”) outperforms a generic “vintage astronomy” title. That is listing advice from the same publisher, not a sales tape.

Risk: public-domain scans are everywhere. A raw Wikimedia file at $39.99 loses to a $4 download of the same plate. The painting has to be a restored composition, with type that is readable at 18×24, which is the standard the repo already set for its own plates. Do not copy a living shop’s recoloring one-for-one.

Already planned: no. Ursa Major is one figure, not this chart.

### 6. Southern sky chart, as the pair to the northern one

The same kind of painting for the southern constellations, sold next to the northern chart.

Why it is the niche cut: northern charts are the default in US and UK listings. A southern companion is the piece a buyer in Australia, New Zealand, or South America cannot get from a “northern zodiac” poster, and it gives a US buyer a second painting for a pair. The evidence is thinner than for the personalized star map. It shows up as an option on northern/southern chart listings, not as a brand with tens of thousands of reviews.

Price range: same as the northern chart, about $15–$40 unframed if it matches those listings. A pair can be offered without discounting each one into the $4 download band.

Target buyer: a southern-hemisphere gift, or someone hanging two maps.

Risk: a southern chart drawn from a northern template with the labels swapped will be wrong. It needs its own plate. Low search volume is the trade for less copycat clutter.

Already planned: no.

### 7. One zodiac sign, as twelve separate paintings

Each sign is its own listing: the figure, the constellation, the name, and the date span. Scorpio is a different search from “constellation print.”

Why the series is the product: [Elistit, May 30, 2026](https://elistit.com/guides/celestial-wall-art-etsy/), says buyers purchase their own sign or a partner’s, and that twelve separate listings get indexed where one listing with twelve variants does not. The guide’s price band for a digital sign print is about $3–$5, and about $15–$22 for a full set of twelve. No shop-level unit sales were pulled for a single sign in this pass. The gift logic is the signal: a birthday has a sign, and a generic Ursa Major plate does not answer that search.

Price range: digital singles about $3–$7 in that guide; a POD paper painting would need to sit near the other art posters, about $20–$40 for 8×10 through 18×24, or it will be compared with the $4 file. Square 8×8 to 12×12 fits a wheel; 16×20 and 18×24 fit a portrait figure. [SnapToSize’s celestial size notes](https://snaptosize.com/etsy-celestial-wall-art-sizes) say that split. It is a template vendor’s page, not sales data.

Target buyer: a birthday gift, usually one sign, sometimes the pair of two people’s signs. [Elistit](https://elistit.com/guides/celestial-wall-art-etsy/) describes the celestial buyer as mostly women 22–40, plus partners buying a gift. Treat that as the guide’s audience note.

Risk: gold linework on navy is the crowded look the same guide names as the default. A vintage figure on the warm paper the repo already uses is the open lane. Twelve listings is twelve files to keep consistent. Astrology-prediction copy (“this sign will…”) is a claim the painting does not need.

Already planned: no.

### 12. Sun and moon as a pair

Two paintings meant to hang together: a sun face and a moon face, engraving or line work, same paper and same frame size.

Why it is included: [Elistit, May 30, 2026](https://elistit.com/guides/celestial-wall-art-etsy/), lists paired sun-and-moon faces as a recurring bedroom set. No unit sales were found for a specific pair in this pass. The signal is weaker than items 1–3. It is here because a pair raises the order above a single $39.99 poster without inventing a new theme.

Price range: if each matches the live posters, about $40 each, or a set near $70–$80. The guide’s digital bundles are much cheaper ($10–$14 for three or four files) and are not the POD price.

Target buyer: a bedroom, bought as a couple of prints rather than as a chart.

Risk: faces of the sun and moon are the most copied motif in the niche. An original drawing in the repo’s cream-paper engraving style is the bar. A clip-art pair is not worth listing.

Already planned: no.

## Rank for the paintings page

Ranked for what should be on this page next, using the sales evidence above. The three live posters stay. They are not the top of the evidence.

1. **Personalized night-sky star map** (item 1). Strongest evidence: a brand page with 42,050 company reviews at a $39 print, a premium competitor at $130–$250, and an Etsy bestseller badge on the same gift. Not on the page. Needs a proof step before Printify. Start with one occasion line, wedding or “the night we met,” so the listing matches how people search.
2. **The moon on one named night** (item 2). Same gift buyer, one date, and it is not the eight-phase chart already live. Weaker unit evidence than the star map. Pair it with the star map so one order can be both.
3. **Twelve zodiac paintings, one listing each** (item 7). The birthday search the current plates do not answer. Evidence is a 2026 seller guide plus the shape of the listings, not a verified unit count. Use the vintage figure style already in the files, not gold-on-navy.
4. **Full northern sky chart, with a southern companion** (items 5 and 6). This is the constellation painting people are shown when they search star maps. Ursa Major and the unlisted Orion plate are details of it. Southern is the niche half.
5. **Eight-phase moon chart** (item 3). Already live, and it is the one current poster that matches a broad search. Keep it. The first photo has to read as a finished painting, because cheap laminated charts own the low end of the same words.

Ursa Major and the Cellarius solar system stay as the plates already in print. They are not the next two to design. Orion waits until a named-figure series exists; listing it alone does not follow the stronger signal. The year-moon poster is a $10 calendar business with stale Amazon badges, a poor fit for a $39.99 art poster. The antique moon drawing is a reasonable later plate if a public-domain drawing is in hand. The sun-and-moon pair is last.
